# Game guide

What there is to do in Unicorn Academy the Fan Game, how the controls work and what a parent can change. The rules of the simulation are in the code and in `tests/domain`, and this guide says what a player meets.

## The herd

| Unicorn | Magic | What it does to a prop that is close by |
|---|---|---|
| Wildstar | Light | Lights lanterns and crystals |
| Leaf | Nature | Makes a flowerbed bloom |
| Glacier | Ice | Grows ice flowers on the ice display |
| River | Water | Raises the fountain spout and sprays flowerbeds |
| Cinder | Fire | Lights braziers |
| Storm | Lightning | Spins pinwheels and makes weather ornaments glow |

Wildstar starts in the forest. The other five wait in clearings there and join the herd the first time Wildstar walks up to them or they are tapped. The herd then follows through every door. Once all five have joined they always follow in the same order, Leaf, Glacier, River, Cinder and Storm, whichever order they were found in. A counter at the top left shows how many friends are still to find and goes away when they are all found. When the last friend joins, a banner and confetti play, the unicorns run in together and each casts a firework of its own.

Magic that switches things on (lanterns, crystals, braziers and flowerbeds) switches them off again after ten seconds.

## The places

There are twenty-five. The forest leads to the palace garden, which has a fountain with two statues and two cups, a gardener, a farm with eight crop beds, a twenty-four tree orchard, a pond and an east meadow. The garden has doors to:

- the palace hall, with a throne for Wildstar and nine more rooms: a dining room, a tea room, a long gallery, a library, a music room, a ballroom, a flower room, a bedroom and a jewel room. In every one of the ten rooms a button at the bottom left, **Go outside**, takes the herd back to the lawn in front of the palace door in one tap, and in every room someone is at work: a steward and a guard in the hall, a cook and a butler in the dining room, a butler in the tea room, a guard in the gallery, a librarian, a maestro in the music room, a dancer in the ballroom, a florist, a nanny in the bedroom and a jeweller;
- the treasure house, with gold, gems, diamonds, money and two chests, and a falling treasure game that lasts sixty seconds and cannot be lost;
- the stable, with a worker who carries fruit, boxed unicorns in their own coats, crates of apples, carrots and sky berries, and a brushing station;
- the playground, with fireworks, spell cannons, a gem fountain, morph machines, confetti poppers, bubbles, balloons and a rainbow slide. When the dance party there reaches its finale, the herd goes on to a dance floor of lit glass with ten stage lights and the toys of the playground standing round it.

A hill in the north of the forest has a mouth in it, and behind the mouth is the crystal cave: lilac stone, a pool that glows turquoise, a hut that is a little spooky and never scary, a bubbling cauldron and twelve sleeping crystals. In its Heart Hall the Heart Crystal goes slowly through every colour of the rainbow. Ten kinds of gem grow in clusters, and a tap on a cluster lets two or three fall, which the herd gathers by walking over them. Each friend's magic does something there, and whatever it changed goes back by itself.

The garden's east gate opens on the magic river. It runs from the palace to the sea, and its water glitters and is shot with violet. It is full of flat stones near enough to step from one to the next, so the herd hops across wherever a child points, and the sparkle follows each hoof. On the far bank stands the Magic Hall, a huge glittering house that only Wildstar may enter. Inside, spell books hover over their lecterns and turn their pages, star globes spin, candles float, two cauldrons bubble, three chandeliers hang and glowing mushrooms grow in the corners, and every one of them answers a tap with its own sparkle. The plants round it glitter when they are tapped. Where the river meets the sea a sandy path leads to the beach.

The beach is the island's north shore. The sea glitters. The herd can wade into the shallows and swim farther out. A tap on a dug-over patch of sand digs up shells, a tap on a clam opens it and rolls out a pearl, and a tap on a plot of smooth sand builds a sandcastle one stage at a time, with a fourth tap to sweep it away and begin again. There are shells and starfish to collect, umbrellas, buckets, driftwood and palms, and a lighthouse on the east dunes.

In the north-east of the forest a gate in the trees opens on Santa's grove, where it is always winter and always Christmas, whatever the calendar says. A trodden trail runs between candy-cane lamp posts to a round square with a tall Christmas tree and presents at its foot, and a tap on a present opens it. Santa and his elves walk between the tree, the cabin, and the sleigh with its reindeer, and a field of snowmen stands to the south-east. A gentle winter tune of its own plays there.

The cabin in Santa's grove has a door that opens at a tap, and glows as it does. Inside, a fire roars in a stone hearth under a wreath and three stockings, and a tap on it sends up sparks. There is a red armchair, a table with a plate of cookies for Santa, his sack of presents, a bench and two shelves of toys, four presents to open and a Christmas tree in the corner with its lights on. Two elves go between the bench, the presents and the tree. Santa himself stays out by the sleigh. The door in the back wall leads back to the grove.

The hut in the crystal cave has a door that opens. Inside, the witch's hut is a good deal bigger than it looks. There is a green rug from the door, a bubbling cauldron, a crystal ball, shelves of jars that clink, two beds of leaves, and the witch, who stirs, looks into her ball and takes down a jar.

The map button at the top right shows the whole island as a child would draw it, with a button for every place. A tap on one takes the herd there at once. The palace is one pin that opens its rooms, so a room is one more tap away.

The forest also holds a Spooky Manor, a dark purple house with glowing orange windows and friendly jack-o-lanterns. Its door asks for a secret word, which is the name of the unicorn who leads. Only Wildstar goes in. Inside is the Wonder Gallery, with statues from other worlds, and a tap on one opens a postcard from the world it comes from. A sparkling butterfly opens a secret lift to the Sky Garden.

Fruit and vegetables fall when a crop bed or a fruit tree is tapped. They lie there until a unicorn walks over them, and a basket under the counter keeps count. Birds, bugs, frogs, ducks, fish and tiny fairies wander about, and each can be tapped. Round arrows at the edges of the screen point to the exits, and a button near the palace shows the whole palace at once.

A violet piano stands on the lawn by the palace road. A tap on it plays a run of notes over its keys, and then the tone matrix opens. It is a song of three parts that play together in one loop, a melody, a beat and a bass, and each part is its own sixteen by sixteen lights that are painted on and rubbed out with a finger.

## The seasons

The world has four seasons. Automatic, the setting a new device starts with, follows the calendar of the device: spring from March, summer from June, autumn from September and winter from December. Winter lays snow on the ground and the scenery and sends the small creatures away. Autumn turns the leaves and lets some fall, and spring brings blossom. The start screen and the Play page of the parent controls pick a season by hand, and `?season=winter` at the end of the address shows one for that visit only. The halls, the houses, the stable and the cave are roofed, so they look the same all year.

## Learning

Learning mode is on for a new device and a parent can turn it off on the start screen or under Learning in the parent controls. With it off nothing opens a card and the voice is silent. The reading computer and the memory and maths tables still open when they are tapped, because a tap is the child asking to play.

**Spelling.** A tap on a creature, a heap of treasure, a friend, or a thing in the palace, the manor or the sky garden shows a card with the thing's picture and asks for its name on an on-screen keyboard. On a new word the key that is needed shines, a wrong key only bounces, nothing says wrong, and the card can always be closed. The help shrinks as a word is shown to be known, and the word comes back later to be sure. A spelled word flies into the Spell Book, a button under the unicorn counter, which has a page for each part of the world and shows what has been found in colour and what has not as shapes that say how many letters the word has. A shape that has not been found yet is a button too: a tap sets a bubble over the thing that gives the word, and each tap on the bubble takes the herd one place along the road.

**Reading.** A big computer stands on the lawn east of the palace. A round is seven things to read. A word is drawn as tiles, one for each group of letters that makes a sound (`sh` and `igh` are one tile each), and they are touched in order. Each says its sound as it is touched. The tiles join into the word, the voice says it, and the reader says it out loud. The computer cannot hear, so the reader says yes by tapping a big tick. A sentence is touched word by word and then read all together. Nothing says wrong, nothing is locked, and any level can be played from the levels map. The course has 85 small stages in 13 chapters, from `cat hat mat sat in it` to Unicorn Land, with 608 sentences. Each level pays a prize that is said before it is played.

**The tables.** The memory table is a game of cards: a deal puts 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24 or 32 pictures on two cards each (6 to 64 cards, which the board shrinks to fit the screen), and two cards are turned at a time. The abacus is the maths table. A switch at the top of the tiles chooses between Level 0 and Level 1. Level 0 has ten kinds of question (counting, what comes next, more and fewer, adding, taking away, tens and ones, doubles and halves, shapes, the clock and patterns). Level 1 has the sums from school: adding up, subtracting, times tables, dividing and mixed sums such as 2 + 7 − 3. Every kind has three steps, shown as the dots on its tile, and a round of five that goes well moves it up one. Level 1 is never locked, and the table opens at Level 1 once a round of it has been finished. A sum with more than one sign is read from the left, and a times sign only comes at the start of it, so the answer is the same by either rule. Both tables pay one, two or three stars and never take one back. At the abacus the child has two switches of their own in the top bar. One turns the voice off at the table, which leaves the parent's voice setting alone, and one swaps the choices for a calculator keypad on the questions whose answer is a number, so they can type it in (digits on the keys or the keyboard, rub out, and a tick to send). A number that is not the answer rests the same way a wrong choice does, and after two of them the keys of the answer shine.

**The voice.** Every word, letter sound and sentence is a recorded clip made once by a speech model, so it sounds the same on every device. The clips are kept on the device as they are heard, and a parent can keep all of them at once under Offline.

## Controls

A child needs only a finger.

- Tap open ground to walk there. Press and hold open ground, or use the arrow keys or WASD, to steer.
- Tap a unicorn who has joined to cast its magic. Tap one who has not joined yet to say hello.
- Tap a cup, door, chest, brush station or piece of treasure to walk over and use it.
- Tap the gardener or the stable worker and they wave.
- Walking into a doorway takes the whole herd through.
- The speaker button turns sound off and on. A round button next to it, or the F key, makes the game full screen.
- The book button opens the Spell Book. On the spell card a real keyboard types the letters, and Escape closes the card.

The start screen has four switches above Play. **Start from scratch** (No at first) opens the game as a visit that begins again and leaves the saved world alone. **Learning mode** (Yes at first) turns spelling and reading on or off. **Start at** (Forest at first) says where the game begins, the forest or the palace. **Season and snow** (Automatic at first) picks Spring, Summer, Autumn or Winter by hand, or lets the calendar choose, and a tap on the snowflake brings the snow at once, before December. A tap on the lit side of a two-sided switch turns it over. A look around with `?incognito` has no Start from scratch switch, since it never touches the saved world.

## Parent controls

The gear button opens the parent controls with one tap, so a child who plays alone is never stuck behind a held button. Anything that cannot be undone, such as starting over, asks again before it does it. With a keyboard, focus the gear and press Enter or Space. Inside the controls, Tab and Shift+Tab stay on the sheet and Escape closes it. A small dot on the gear means something needs a parent's eye, such as a save that failed or a Spell Book that cannot be kept on this device, and the controls open on that page.

| Page | What it is for |
|---|---|
| Sound | Music and effects volume, sound off, and reduced effects |
| Play | Whether each visit starts from scratch, where it starts, which season it is, how close the picture is, full screen, and low-end mode |
| Learning | Whether learning is on, how much help new words get, the keyboard and the letters, how often the game asks, whether the words and the letters pressed are said aloud, and whether friends join by name. It also shows how the child is doing word by word and whether the browser has promised to keep the Spell Book, saves a copy of the book as a file, adds one back, and starts a new book |
| Saving | Where progress is kept, whether it is safe, and a download of a copy when it is not |
| Offline | Whether the game is kept for play with no connection, how many spoken lessons are kept, and how to switch to a newer version |
| Recovery | Download a copy of the world, load a copy from a file, go back to an earlier copy, or start a brand-new world |
| About | The version, the licences and the notices of the works in the game |

A tablet that drops frames can play in low-end mode. The Play page has Automatic, On and Off, and Automatic turns it on for a Kindle Fire only. Low-end mode draws one pixel for each pixel of the page, leaves out scenery that is off the screen, throws about two thirds of the sparkles and gives the 32 facings to Wildstar alone, where the standard mode turns every unicorn through 32. The followers keep the eight facings of their first sheet. A device that says it has 2 GB of memory is given the same, and one with less keeps the eight for everyone. `src/render/profile.ts` holds every difference in one table.

## Keeping progress

Progress is written to IndexedDB on the device first, and the game never waits for the network. The device keeps the ten newest snapshots. A snapshot that fails validation is set aside and the game falls back to the newest one that passes. Device settings such as volume stay on the device.

Whenever a parent action replaces the world (start over, load a file, go back to an earlier copy), the world it replaces is put aside first, and eight such copies are kept. Under Recovery:

- Download a copy saves the world as a JSON file.
- Choose a file loads a downloaded copy. A file that is not a world is refused with a reason, and nothing changes.
- Earlier copies lists the worlds that were put aside. Use this copy brings one back.
- Start over begins a new world after putting the current one aside.

The Spell Book is kept apart from the world, in a store of its own, so a new world, or a visit that starts from the beginning, does not clear it. A look around with `?incognito` shows the book and writes nothing to it. Under Learning, Save a copy writes the book to a file and Add a saved Spell Book puts a file together with the book on the device. Adding never takes a word or a star away.

Browsers can clear what a site stores when a device runs short of space. Adding the game to the Home Screen and saving a copy now and then protects against that. To move to another device, download a copy of the world and the Spell Book on the old one and add them on the new one.

## Offline play

The first visit downloads what is needed to play and keeps the rest of the game in the background. When the Offline page says the game is ready, it plays with no connection. The spoken lessons are not part of that download. A lesson is kept the first time it is heard, so the voice works offline for everything the child has met, and the Offline page has a button to keep all of them before a journey.

To install on an iPhone, open the game in Safari while online, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, and play from the new icon. An iPhone can keep a Home Screen app's saves apart from Safari's, so progress made in a Safari tab does not appear in the app until it is moved across with a copy from Recovery. When a newer version is available, the Offline page offers to switch to it, and the saved herd carries over.
