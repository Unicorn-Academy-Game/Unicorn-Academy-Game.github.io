# The voice

The game's voice is a set of short clips of speech, one for every line the game can say. They are made here, once, by a speech model, and they ship with the game as ordinary sound files. The game never asks a phone to speak, so the voice is the same on every device, it works with no network once the game is saved, and nothing is read out in a voice a parent has not heard.

The voice is Qwen3-TTS (`mlx-community/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-Base-bf16`, Apache 2.0), cloning two reference clips in `refs/` that were designed with the VoiceDesign model from a written brief and picked by ear from seven candidate voices: `calm.wav` for ordinary lines and `cheer.wav` for cheers, milestones, finished pages and shouts. Before that the voice was Kokoro `af_heart`, which was found weird; this one was picked blind from seven candidates ("no contest"). `generate_qwen.py` makes the clips now; `generate.py` and the Kokoro machinery stay because the measures, listeners and tests are written against them, and the new generator reuses those parts. Word times come from Qwen3-ForcedAligner hearing each kept sentence. The clips are made at the pace the model gives a line for a child, and the packer slows them again as it packs them (below), so a sentence is spoken at about 2.7 words a second, as one speaks to a child who is learning to read.

## What is here

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `lines.json` | Every clip the game can ask for, with the words as the card shows them and, where the voice must be told something different to say it well, the words as they are spoken. The game writes it (`pnpm voice:lines`) and a test fails when it is out of date. |
| `generate_qwen.py` | Makes the clips with the Qwen3 voice. Same masters and report shape as `generate.py`, so the packer, the word-times export and the checks work unchanged. |
| `requirements-qwen.txt` | The Python packages for the Qwen3 generator, pinned. It needs MLX, so Apple Silicon. |
| `refs/` | The two reference clips the voice is cloned from: `calm.wav` and `cheer.wav`. |
| `generate.py` | Made the clips with the old Kokoro voice. Kept because its measures, cutting and tests are the quality bar the new generator is judged against. |
| `sounds.py` | What each letter sounds like, and how the sound is cut out of a syllable or a word. `test_sounds.py` tests the cutting and the rule for when a sound is understood. |
| `group_sounds.py` | What each group of letters sounds like (sh, ch, th, oo, uu, ow, oy, aw, ar, or, er, air, ng, nk, ing), for the tiles of the reading computer. Each is cut from a word the way a letter's sound is, in one of four shapes: a consonant with a breath of vowel after it, a vowel from where it begins to where its word ends, a vowel from the middle of a word made longer, or the end of a word. `test_group_sounds.py` tests the cutting and the rule for when a sound is understood, with a stand-in for the model. |
| `tiles.py`, `tiles-ok.json` | Asks of every word of the reading course whether what a child hears as she touches its tiles is the word the voice says. Each tile says a sound, the sounds one after the other are set beside the phonemes Misaki gives the word, and a word whose tiles say something else is named. `tiles-ok.json` lists, with the reason, a word where a person looked and decided the tiles are right, and it is empty. `test_tiles.py` tests the rule. |
| `cuts.py`, `phones.py` | How the words of a list are cut out of one breath, and what kind of sound a line begins and ends with. A word is cut at the quietest place between it and the next, and between two words that would run together the model is given a word of no use that is shut in silence at both ends. `test_cuts.py` tests it. |
| `frames.py` | How a clip that is said beside others in a question (`lead.34`, `last.5`, `lead.plus`) is cut out of a whole sentence: the sentence in `lines.json` as its `frame`, the quietest place between the piece and its neighbours, and the level of the sentence that every piece keeps. `test_frames.py` tests it. |
| `questions.py`, `export-questions.ts`, `mos.py` | Listen to the maths game's questions as the game says them, from the files it plays: the clips of a question laid end to end with the quiet the game leaves (`export-questions.ts` writes it from the game's own `GAP` and `runsOn`), and for each question its length, its dead air, how natural a learned predictor finds it (`mos.py`, UTMOS22) and how many words the word listener got wrong. `test_questions.py` tests the laying out. |
| `times.py` | When each word of a recorded sentence begins and ends. The model gives every sound a length of its own before it speaks and builds the audio from them, so the time of a word is the sum of the lengths before it, exact for the take that was kept. The report keeps the times, and the game lights each word as the voice reaches it. `test_times.py` tests it. |
| `export-course.ts` | Prints the reading course as JSON for the Python tools: every word with its tiles and every sentence with its words. |
| `export-times.ts`, `times-json.ts` | Write `src/content/reading/line-times.json` from the report (`pnpm voice:times`), or with `--check` fail when the file is not what the report says. |
| `speak.py` | The model: phonemes in, audio and the time each phoneme starts out. Everything that decides how a clip sounds is pinned here: the weights by revision, the voice by name, and the noise by seed. |
| `judge.py` | Two listeners that are not the speaker: a speech recogniser that writes down the words and a phoneme recogniser that writes down the sounds. It also holds the rule for when a clip is understood, and the other ways a letter or a word may be written. |
| `dsp.py` | Cutting, fading, and the measures: how loud the ends are, how voiced the clip is, where the formants of a vowel are, and where a hiss follows the voice. |
| `test_dsp.py`, `test_judge.py` | Tests of the cutting, made on tones and noise, and of the rule for when a clip is understood, made on what the listeners wrote. Run them with `python -m unittest discover -s tools/voice -p 'test_*.py'`. |
| `review.py` | Draws the masters as sheets of spectrograms, for a person to look through. |
| `verify.py` | Has the two listeners hear the MP3 files the game plays, to see that the loudness setting and the encoder left every clip as clear as its master. |
| `overrides.json` | A hand choice for a line, in one of four forms. `{"word.frog": {"style": 8, "seed": 3, "speed": 0.9}}` picks one take (the speed is optional). `{"word.bee": {"copy": "letter.b"}}` makes the line the clip of another line, so the two cannot differ. `{"word.the": {"phonemes": "ðˈʌ."}}` gives the sounds the line is spoken with, and the line is still spoken in six ways and judged like any other. A take and phonemes can be given together, and the line is then spoken in that one way with those sounds. `{"last.8": {"frame": "What is two plus [eight]?"}}` gives a piece of a sentence another sentence to be cut from, with the same piece in it (the generator stops when it is not the same). It holds sixteen. The shout for ruby is a take picked by hand, because at the pace of a shout the model opens it with a long r that the word listener hears as "or", and the same take a little faster is heard cleanly. The word bee is a copy of the letter b, because the model says the lone word "bee" as "bay" or ends its vowel with a breath, and the letter b is the same sound and comes out clean every time. The word are is a copy of the letter r for the same reason, and for another: of forty takes of the lone word, one was understood, and that one had a `d` in front of it and a `t` after it, which is the puff of air of the word before and the word after it in the list. The word for is a copy of the word four, which is the same word to the ear and comes out clear. The model says the lone word `for` with the weak vowel of a word that only links two others: the sound listener wrote `f a k` for the take the generator chose, the second formant of its vowel lies between 1,467 and 1,917 Hz where that of `four` lies between 847 and 1,066 Hz, and the file the game would have played was heard as "Frick". The shouts for shell, splash and moon and the word splash are takes picked by hand too. In the lists the recogniser heard shell as "Ishel" and splash as "It's splash", and the shout for moon ended loud. Each was found by speaking the line alone at three speeds, twelve rows of style and two seeds, and one was taken from the middle of a run of takes that were all understood. The three that give phonemes are words that Misaki writes in a way that is not the word as a person says it alone. It gives `catch` as `kˈɛʧ`, which says "ketch", and `an` as `ɐn`, a weak vowel that is not the one in the name of the letter. It gives `the` as `ðə`, which the model speaks in 0.12 seconds, under the 0.15 that the check of the speech pack asks of a clip, so it is given the stressed form that a teacher says, `ðˈʌ`. The words saw and fawn are given a take and phonemes together. With Misaki's `ɔ` none of forty takes of either was understood, and the word listener heard "Suck." for saw and "Fun." for fawn. After a hiss the model says `ɔ` as an open vowel (the master of saw has its first two formants near 970 and between 1,500 and 1,700 Hz, where the master of dawn has 560 and 1,200), and at the pace of a word it puts a voiced sound before the s of saw that the sound listener writes as `i s aː`. The rounder `ɒ` is understood: saw at speed 1.0 is heard as "saw." on three seeds of three, and fawn at the pace of a word is written `f ɑː n` on three of three. Both are therefore a take picked by hand with `ɒ`, and `tiles-ok.json` says why their tiles still count as right. The number eighty is the fourteenth. With Misaki's `ɔ` one take in forty was understood, and the master of that one was heard as "wow", "whoa" or "well" when it was decoded from its file, when it was a few decibels louder or quieter, and when it was encoded, so it was on the edge and would have shipped as another word. The same sounds with a long vowel, `wˈɔːl.`, were understood in two takes of sixteen, and the take kept is heard as "wall?" at every setting tried: as the master, 4 dB either way, as MP3 at quality 2 and 5, and slowed by 1.15 and 1.25. The vowel of saw, `ɒ`, was tried first and understood in none of forty. The sixteenth is `last.8`, the eight that ends "What is five plus eight?": in all ten takes of that sentence the piece cut from it was all low rumble (the generator's check for a rattle found 100% of its voice under 130 Hz in a piece 0.4 seconds long), and the same cut from "What is two plus eight?" is clean. |
| `requirements.txt` | The Python packages, pinned. |

The clips themselves, and what was said about each, are in `assets/source/voice` (16-bit mono at 24 kHz, one `.wav` for each, and `report.json`). `pnpm assets:build` packs them into the files the game plays (see below).

## Making the clips

With the Qwen3 voice (what makes the clips now; MLX needs Apple Silicon):

```
python3.12 -m venv /tmp/ua-qwen/.venv
/tmp/ua-qwen/.venv/bin/pip install -r tools/voice/requirements-qwen.txt
/tmp/ua-qwen/.venv/bin/python tools/voice/generate_qwen.py            # what is missing
/tmp/ua-qwen/.venv/bin/python tools/voice/generate_qwen.py --only 'word.*,letter.r'
/tmp/ua-qwen/.venv/bin/python tools/voice/generate_qwen.py --force    # everything again
```

A run that is stopped keeps what it made: the report is written after every clip, and a clip already in the report with its master on disk is skipped unless `--force` is given. The seeds are fixed, so the same machine makes the same takes again. A run with no `--only` also fills in the word times of every sentence from the aligner. The old Kokoro way, kept for its measures and tests:

```
python3.12 -m venv /tmp/ua-voice/.venv
/tmp/ua-voice/.venv/bin/pip install -r tools/voice/requirements.txt
/tmp/ua-voice/.venv/bin/python tools/voice/generate.py              # what is missing or out of date
/tmp/ua-voice/.venv/bin/python tools/voice/generate.py --only 'word.*,letter.r'
/tmp/ua-voice/.venv/bin/python tools/voice/generate.py --force      # everything again
```

The first run downloads the models (about 2.1 GB). Everything runs on the CPU. The first 218 clips took 50 minutes (3024 s) on an Apple M1 Max with ten cores that other jobs were also using, and the alphabet was about a sixth of that. The 348 clips that came with the hundred words took 51 minutes (3068 s) on the same machine. A clip made again alone takes under a minute.

Do not run `generate.py` with no `--only` until `--list` says what it would make. At the commit that added the pieces below it would have made 25 sounds and 765 lines again, because the fingerprints in the report did not match what the code computes for them, which is hours of the processor spent on clips that were already fine. Nobody has found out yet why they differ. Name what you mean with `--only`.

A run that is stopped keeps what it made, since the report is written after every clip. A clip is made again when its words, its phonemes, the takes it is tried with, or `GENERATOR` in `generate.py` change, so change `GENERATOR` when the way clips are made changes. The seeds are fixed, so the same machine makes the same clips again.

A long run can be split. `--shard K/N` makes every N-th piece of work, the K-th of them, and keeps its report in a file of its own, so that N runs go at once (`--threads` gives each a share of the processor), and `--merge` puts the reports of the shards into the report when none is still going. `--list` says what would be made and stops, and `--new` with `--only` makes only the clips that have no master yet. The words of the reading course were made as four shards side by side and its sentences as two.

When the game says something new, add it in `src/learning/lines.ts`, run `pnpm voice:lines`, run `generate.py`, then `pnpm assets:build` and `pnpm assets:validate`.

The environment lives under `/tmp`, which a machine clears, and on 5 November the one from before had its folders and none of its Python files. Make it again with the commands above. Besides the pinned packages, `misaki` needs the English model of spaCy, `en_core_web_sm`, and a plain install of `requirements.txt` does not bring it. The environment that made the lines for reading has spaCy 3.8.18 and the model 3.8.0 in it. Install the model into the same environment with `python -m spacy download en_core_web_sm`, which runs `pip`. An environment made by `uv` has no `pip` unless it was made with `--seed`.

The lines of the reading computer are the 19 `read.*` clips (a welcome, the three things that are done with a word, the grown-up's line, the cheers for a level, a chapter and the whole course, and the three that were there before), and `verify.py` hears each on the file that ships. The present tense of read in Let's read and You can read it is spoken as "reed", which a word listener cannot tell from "red", so for those two the check is the phoneme listener.

## How a clip is chosen

Every clip is a set of candidates, and the generator keeps the best.

- A line is spoken six times, a little differently each time. The model has a row of style for each length of phonemes, and each take moves along the rows and uses a noise of its own.
- A word, a shout or a cheer is also spoken together with the others of its kind, in one breath, and each is cut out of the breath where it is quietest between it and the next (`cuts.py`). A word is the hardest thing to ask of the model, because it has almost nothing to go on (`VOICES.md` says the voices are weak on utterances of fewer than 10 to 20 tokens, and suggests bundling short ones together, which is what this does). Said alone, it voices the `f` of `fish`, puts a `z` in front of `duck` and says it as `dark`. In a list the model has a rhythm to follow, and more of the words come out right. Between two words that would run together the model is given a carrier (`CARRIERS`), a word of no use to us that is shut in silence at both ends, and the cut is made in that. A carrier ends the breath too, so that no word is the one the model ends on, where it breathes out. The model takes 510 phonemes in one breath, so a kind is spoken in groups of 24 (`GROUP` in `generate.py`), and the hundred words of the Spell Book, which were made the first way, are groups of their own. A group is spoken in orders, four at a time (`PER_ROUND`), the first in the order of the list and the others in shuffles of it, so that a word is cut out beside other neighbours, until each word has two takes that were understood and are clean (below), or sixteen orders have been spoken (`MAX_ORDERS`). The run stops if a group does not fit. A line is made again when its group changes, so the words that were added on the end of the table left the first group as it was.
- A take is measured. A take with a fault is not a good take, so the listeners hear only the takes without a fault, unless there are none.
- A take is understood when the recogniser wrote the words that were meant, or the sounds it wrote are close to the sounds that were meant. A word that sounds like another (`bee`, `berry`, `hay`) may be written either way (`HOMOPHONES` in `judge.py`).
- A take of a word, a shout or a cheer that was understood is also clean when the sound listener wrote no sound before the word or after it that was not meant (`edge_inserts` in `judge.py`, `Verdict.clean` in `generate.py`). A carrier is shut at its end, and as it lets go it puffs, up to 70 ms after the mark where the model says it ends. When the next word starts soon after, the puff is shallower to the next word than the closing is, so the cut is made in the closing and the puff rides on the front of the word. The word listener ignores a puff and says the word. The sound listener writes it as a stop (`b`, `d`, `ɡ`) in front of the word. The puff measured between -20 and -8 dB, so it is a small click in front of the word that nobody has listened to. The generator keeps taking orders until two takes are clean, prefers a clean take to one the listeners scored better, and keeps a take that is not clean only when none is. `verify.py` lists the shipped clips that have such a sound, for a person to hear. What was left was measured on the files that ship, by a one-off script that was not kept, which lays the listener's 20 ms frames over the file. The report holds a sound at an edge for 6 clips that had no clean take (`deck`, `hair`, `hopped`, `house`, `joy` and `sell`), and 67 of the 1,179 words, shouts and cheers had one in the transcription that was stored with the take. In the shipped files 60 of those clips still have one, 67 sounds in all, and `deck`, `giggle`, `howl`, `pear`, `puzzle`, `sail` and `sell` have none, so what the sound listener writes at an edge moves with the level and the encoder. It moves the other way too. The last full run of `verify.py` on the shipped files (1,999 clips heard in 10,987 seconds, none of them not understood) lists 83 clips for a person to hear, which are all 60 of those and 23 more that had no such sound in the transcription stored with their take (21 words, the shout `shout.cinder` and the line `read.told`). Of the 83, 69 are words, 12 are shouts, one is a cheer and one is a line of the screen. Of the 67 sounds measured in the 60, 10 lie in the quiet that the listener puts after the clip, 7 are at the very end of the clip and 48 dB or more under its loudest part, 6 are the short vowel after a final `b` or `g` (`bug`, `tub`, `web`, `rib`, `shrub`, `munch`), which is how a stop lets go, 3 are a `z` after the `v` of `hive` and `ladybug`, 3 are 35 to 43 dB under (`clutch`, `try`, `wear`), and 38 lie in the first 20 to 60 ms of the clip, 3 to 38 dB under its loudest part and most of them near 18. A word that starts on a hiss or a burst starts loud, so those at the start are either the puff or the word's own first sound, and the file cannot say which. A word or a shout that has a second burst of sound after a silence of 90 ms is flagged and not kept (`dsp.bursts`), so none of the words and shouts among them is a click on its own apart from the word, and nobody has listened to any of the 67.
- A word or a sentence that was understood as a master is heard again as the game plays it. The packer sets every clip to one loudness, which turns a clip down by as much as 9 dB or up by 5, and then encodes it as an MP3, and the word listener is sensitive to both: `word.car` was heard as `Car.` from the master and as `Kark` from the file. So the take is put through the packer's own gain (`pack_gain_db` in `generate.py`, which uses `momentary_loudness` in `dsp.py`, a port of `tools/audio/loudness.ts`; it gives the gain that `assets/source/voice/build.json` records to within 0.005 dB for the 65 of 66 clips tried that the limiter did not touch), encoded and decoded by the same ffmpeg command as the packer's (`through_mp3`), and heard at that level and 2 dB either side of it (`as_packed`, `PACKED_OFFSETS`). It is a good take only when it is understood at all three. The take is made into a master and read back as the packer reads it (`master_samples`, which writes it with the call `save` makes and reads the 16-bit integers), turned by the gain, and written as the packer writes it (`packer_samples`, which rounds each sample as `Math.round(v * 32767)` does in `wavBytes`, where the sound file library takes the step below in about half of them), so the encoder is given the packer's own samples. That has to be exact: `word.vet` is on the edge of being understood, and the word listener heard "VET" from the file written the library's way and "That." from the file the packer writes, and the generator had chosen the take on the first. Of 260 clips tried, 236 decoded to the very samples of the file that ships. The 24 that did not are the 21 that the limiter held and three that differ by less than 0.001. The limiter turns a clip down around its peak where `pack_gain_db` turns the whole clip down, so a word the limiter holds is heard a little quieter than the game plays it, and `verify.py` is what settles it. The packed take at the packer's own level is also looked at for a rattle, since the encoder and the gain can bring one out (`word.sell` has 49% of its voice under 130 Hz as a master, which passes, and 58% as the file that shipped, which does not). The letters and the sounds are heard at three fixed levels instead (`LEVELS`), which is how they were checked before and is not what the packer does, but `verify.py` found none of them wanting. Words were checked that way first too, and 24 of the clips that shipped failed `verify.py`, so the level the packer chooses is what counts for a word or a sentence.
- Of the good takes, the one that is most like the others is kept, so an odd take never wins.
- When fewer than two takes are good, up to three more rounds of six are made, along the rows between the first six. A word, a shout or a cheer waits for more orders of its group first, up to sixteen, until two takes are good and clean. If its group has had them all and fewer than two takes were understood, it is spoken alone, six ways and up to three more rounds of six, as a sentence is. If two were understood and fewer than two are clean, the best of them is kept and the report says the sounds at its ends (`edges`).
- A clip with no good take is kept as the best of the bad and marked `review` in the report.

The faults that are looked for are a length outside what the clip kind allows, a peak at full scale, loud sound at either end (a cut-off word), a silence in the middle, two bursts of sound where a word should be one, hardly any voice, and a rattle. The model's voice sits between 160 and 270 Hz, and now and then it says a back vowel at the end of a word (`all`, `were`, `cloud`, the ow of cow) at half that pitch, which is not a low voice and not a pleasant one to hear a lesson in. `dsp.deep_share` is the share of the loud voiced moments of a clip that are under 130 Hz, and a clip with most of its voice there has the fault. A line that begins with a vowel or a burst may begin loud, and a line that ends with one of those or a hiss may end loud.

The model leaves a loud hiss after the last voiced sound of many lines, a breath that is heard as an `s` or a `t` after `apple` or `tomato`. When a line does not end with a burst or a hiss, `dsp.cut_hiss` finds where the voice stops and fades the clip out there.

## The maths questions

A question of the maths game is said by several clips one after the other (`What is`, `thirty-four`, `plus`, `five`). They used to be clips said alone, each with the falling end of a clip that is finished, and the game left 0.3 seconds between them, so a question sounded like a list read out. Measured on the files the game plays, over the 540 questions the game asks at the three levels of its fifteen strands, a question was 3.99 seconds long on average with 0.75 seconds of quiet inside it. Of 48 of them heard by the word listener, it wrote "before" for the number four in four.

A clip that is said next to others is now a piece cut out of a whole sentence, in the place it has in the question (`frames.py`, and the `frame` of the line in `lines.json`, which the game writes from `src/learning/play/maths/clips.ts`). `lead.34` is the 34 of `What is [thirty-four] plus five?`, a piece with more of the sentence after it, and the game plays the next clip with no gap after it (`runsOn` in `src/shared/audio.ts`). `last.5` is the 5 that ends `What is five plus [five]?`. `num.34` is still a clip of its own, said alone, for the buttons of the answers. `maths.how-many` and `maths.in-all` are whole questions, cut from `Count them. [How many?]` because the model says two or three words flatly when it says them with nothing before them. They are packed like any whole clip.

How a piece is made (`make_frame` in `generate.py`):

- The model speaks the whole sentence, up to ten ways (`frame_takes`), and the first way that gives a clean piece is kept, so that the pieces of one question are one voice. The word listener and the sound listener hear the sentence, not the piece, since a piece of a few sounds is not for them to understand: what the piece has to be is what they understood in its place.
- The piece is cut at the quietest place between it and its neighbours (`cuts.cut_marks`, with the times of the words from `times.word_spans`). Only the start and the end of the sentence are held to being quiet. A piece that ends the sentence in voice is cut where the voice stops, as a line is, and the hiss that the model breathes out after it is taken off (`closed`).
- The piece is heard alone by the sound listener, and a sound before or after it that it does not have makes the take not clean, as for a word (`stray_sounds`). Of the 224 pieces of numbers and words, 197 came out the same with the check as without it, 27 are another take, and 13 still have such a sound after ten takes, which the report says in `edges`.
- The sentence is set to -16 LUFS (`SENTENCE_LUFS`), and every piece of it keeps its place in that: the packer turns every piece by one fixed gain (`PIECE_LUFS` in `src/shared/audio.ts`), where a whole clip is turned to -12. A piece that is more than 6 LU under its sentence is turned up to that (`MOST_UNDER`), because the end of a question falls away and the end of a sentence at the pace of a child's lesson can be unheard. In the pack the pieces are between -17.8 and -11.0 LUFS, and `tools/assets/check/speech.ts` allows 7 LU under the target and 1 LU over for a piece (a word that is voiced from end to end measures hotter than the sentence it came from, whose loudest 400 ms holds its pauses). Heard as a whole, the loudest 400 ms of the 540 questions are between -12.4 and -11.2 LUFS (median -12.0), where the questions of the old clips were all within 0.2 of -12.0.

It is not only the pieces: of the 21 questions `What is N take away 2?`, for N from 0 to 20, the word listener writes the sentence the model says whole as "takeaway" in one word in 2, and the pieces of the game in 7. In four of those seven it is the same one word (`Zero Takeaway`), which the whole sentences show too. In the other three it is another word for the number: `Tube` for two, `Forbes` for four and `Ape` for eight. The sound listener hears the four clean, a stray `b` after the two, and a stray `f` and a doubled `t` around the eight. The check of a piece alone does not find these, since `lead.2` and `lead.8` are clean on their own, so they are in the join. They are not understood and there is no fix for them yet. Nobody has listened to any of it.

Every measure here is a stand-in. UTMOS is a network trained on what adults rated, and nobody has checked it against children or against speech slowed as this is. It told a sentence that runs on from a list, which it did, and is not a measure of how a child hears it. The lowest scores that are left are `What comes next?` (the numbers before it are clips said alone, with the quiet of a list, as a count should be) and the questions of the mixed strand with `take away` in them, and `How many?` is 3.93, up from 3.25. Listen to a question of each strand before shipping it. Two other ways were tried on a bench and not taken: slowing a sentence with an overlap-add of the pitch periods (Praat) in place of ffmpeg's `atempo`, which gave back 0.15 to 0.45 UTMOS on 3 of the 12 sentences tried, and a larger model (Ming-3.0-tts, MIT licence, which ranks above Kokoro on a public arena but needs a pinned torch and gives no times for words, which the lit words of the reading computer need), which is the one to try if the pieces are not enough.

## The letters

A letter is a different job, because the model says a lone letter badly, with a click at the start and a vowel that is a little wrong. Each letter is made two ways, and the best take of either is kept.

- In a list. The model says the whole alphabet in one breath, in eight orders, and each letter is cut out where the model says it begins and ends. Every letter is said the same plain way. The model opens its vowels more in a list.
- Alone. The model says the name of the letter with a full stop after it, six ways. When fewer than two takes pass, up to eighteen more are made, six at a time.

Letters have their own measures, because a recogniser reads sounds as words and will take a wrong vowel for the right word. The model's own plain words (`bee`, `kay`, `eye`, `go`, `cue`, `eff`) are the yardstick, and the measures are wide on purpose: where the second formant of the vowel is by its end and in its middle, how open the vowel of `eff`, `em` and `ess` is, and how loud a consonant at the start of a name is. A name that begins with a vowel is cut at the very place the vowel rises, because what the model breathes in front of it is heard as a consonant (`thee` for `e`). A lone vowel has the glide in front of it cut away.

The names are the names of the letters, not their sounds. `r` is said as the name of the letter, `are`. The game no longer plays them, since a key she presses says its sound (see below), but they stay in the list: the word `bee` is a copy of `letter.b`, and a parent switch for names instead of sounds would need them.

With the Qwen3 voice a letter is made by cloning its name said alone: first as a lone capital with a full stop (`A.`), then written out (`Ay.`), three seeds of each, judged by the sound listener against the name's sounds (the `NAMES` table, copied into `generate_qwen.py`) and the other ways a letter may be written. The vowel of the take that is kept is measured and held in the report. A letter no take satisfies is marked `review`. The copies in `overrides.json` belong to the Kokoro pipeline; the Qwen3 generator speaks every word itself.

## The sounds

A key she presses says what its letter sounds like, `buh` for `b` and `eh` for `e`, and not its name. These are the `sound.*` clips, one for each letter, made by `sounds.py` and judged in `generate.py` (`make_sounds`).

The model cannot say a sound by itself. Given `b` alone it gives a click and `buh`, given `s` it gives a word, and given a lone vowel it reads another one (tried on all 26, and only `n` and `s` had a lone take that the sound listener understood). It says a syllable or a plain word well, so each sound is cut out of one where the model says the sound begins and ends:

- A vowel is cut from a plain word (`sat`, `bed`, `sit`, `hot`, `sun`, four words for each, and each also with the vowel drawn out), from the middle 70 percent of the vowel, and lengthened with Praat's overlap-add (at most three times, which is its limit). A take is kept only when the word it came from was heard by the word listener, so `sat` that came out as `sit` is not a source of `a`. The sound listener cannot tell the five short vowels apart in a tenth of a second (it writes most of them as `a`), so it only has to hear a vowel. The short i is the exception, because a child told that `i` says `uh` hears `a`, `u` and `i` as one sound. Its first and second formant are measured (`dsp.vowel`) and a take is kept only when they are between those of the model's plain short i (about 760 and 2,160 Hz) and its `ee` (about 500 and 2,450): `BETWEEN` in `sounds.py`, 540 to 690 and 2,220 to 2,500, and its three extra source words (`lid`, `kid`, `tin`) give the choice of takes more to pick from. The range is part of the recipe, so changing it makes the clip again.
- A consonant is the sound and a tenth of a second of the vowel after it, cut from `Cuh`: `buh`, `fuh`, `luh`. A held sound (`sss`, `mmm`) was tried first, by saying the sound several times over and cutting the middle, and the listener understood about one take in a hundred (1 of 144 for `s`, 1 for `m`, 3 for `n`, none for `f` or `z`), so it was dropped. The sound listener must write the consonant, and may write a vowel after it.
- The listener writes `z` as `s`, and cannot tell `f` from `v`. Those are told apart by the hum of the voice under the hiss, which is measured: below 500 Hz against above 2 kHz is at most -8 dB for `s` and -6 dB for `f`, and at least -3 dB for `z` and 3 dB for `v` (the model's own takes sit at -26 to -18, -16 to -9, 1 to 4.5 and 12 to 18).

Like the rest of the voice, a person has not heard these. The two things to listen for are the vowels, which are the model's own open vowels (an `e` that sounds like `a`, an `o` that sounds like `u`) and could not be checked by machine, and the `uh` after each consonant, which is meant to be short. Write down any letter that sounds wrong and make it again with `generate.py --only 'sound.e'`.

That is how the Kokoro voice cut them. The Qwen3 voice makes the sounds in `generate_qwen.py` and cuts them by the sound listener's own timings. A vowel is cut out of a plain word the voice says well (`sat`, `bed`, `sit`, `hot`, `sun` and their neighbours): the word has to be heard as itself, the middle of its vowel is kept and lengthened to 0.4 s. A consonant is cloned as its syllable (`buh`) and cut where the listener's first vowel has had a tenth of a second (0.08 s for `t` and `y`). `ng`, `nk` and `ing` are cut from the ends of words (`ring`, `pink`), `x` from the end of `box`, `fox`, `six` or `axe`, `th` and `ch` are made as syllables because they cannot start their tile phrase, and the other groups are cut out of the phrase of their tile ("oo, as in moon") where the aligner hears the first word. The hum ranges above were measured on Kokoro and do not separate in this voice, so the hum is recorded in the report and decides nothing. What tells the pairs the listener writes as one apart is how much of the consonant is voiced, measured on this voice: `s` about 0.08 of it, `z` and `v` all of it, `g` about 0.55 against `k` at none, `j` about 0.35 against `ch` at none. The short i's formant range from the Kokoro voice is a preference in the choice of takes, not a gate, because this voice's own short i sits a little lower. A sound no take satisfies is marked `review`, and making one again is `generate_qwen.py --only 'sound.e'`.

## The reading course

The reading computer needs three kinds of clip that the rest of the game does not. The generator learns what they are from the same list as everything else (`lines.json`, written by `pnpm voice:lines` from `src/learning/course-clips.ts`), and `export-course.ts` hands it the course itself. There are 2,556 clips in all, 24.58 MiB as shipped, 4,046 seconds of speech.

- **A word of the course.** A `word.*` clip like the hundred of the Spell Book, made in lists and cut at the quietest place between two words (`cuts.py`). The course has 945 words to sound out and 81 that cannot be, and with the words the two share there are 1,075 word clips.
- **The sound of a tile.** A tile is a group of letters, and 15 of the groups are not a letter (`group_sounds.py`, 41 `sound.*` clips with the 26 letters). A tile in a word that cannot be sounded out says the name of its letter (`letter.*`), and every other tile says its sound (`sound.*`).
- **A sentence.** Each of the 398 is one `line.*` clip, spoken whole and not put together from words, because a sentence in one breath has the rhythm of speech and words one after the other do not (inferred from how the model speaks lists, not heard side by side by a person). It is spoken four ways, at the pace of its own length, and the best take is kept like any other. How long it may be goes by its words (`line_bounds` in `generate.py`).

A word whose tiles would teach her a sound the word does not have is found before it is spoken. `tiles.py` runs over the course (`python tools/voice/tiles.py`), sets what each word's tiles say beside the phonemes Misaki gives it, and names a word that differs. The fix is in the course (a word that cannot be sounded out goes among the tricky words, as `walk`, `talk` and `walked` did, or is taken out, as `purr` was, so the course lost four words of the 949 it first had and gained three tricky ones) or in the clip (the overrides above).

The time of each word of a sentence comes from the model and not from a recogniser. The model is given a length for every sound before it speaks and builds the audio from them, so a word begins where the lengths before it add up to (`times.py`), and that is exact for the take that was kept, which the times a recogniser gives are not. `verify.py` checks them with the word listener afterwards, and `src/content/reading/line-times.json` is a copy of them that `pnpm voice:times` writes from the report. The game lights each word as the voice reaches it.

Five clips are marked `review` in the report, and a person should hear them before anyone else does. `can.wildstar` is heard by the word listener exactly as written ("Can you spell Wildstar?") and is flagged only for beginning loud (-15 dB, the burst of its `c`). `word.an` is heard as the letter name "N" with the sounds `ʔ aː n`; `word.shore` is heard as "Sure" and `word.wore` as "War", which are the words' own near-homophones and may be the words said right. `sound.ch` is written `tɕh` by the sound listener, a `ch` with its breath, which its rule does not accept. Every other clip of the 2,556 was understood by the listeners in the take that was kept, and every one of the 608 sentences was aligned for its word times with no sentence needing the fallback split. Nobody has listened to the five. Nobody has listened to the 15 sounds of groups of letters either, and the vowels among them (`oo`, `uu`, `ow`, `oy`, `aw`, `ar`, `or`, `er`, `air`) were checked by formants and by the phoneme listener only, which is weak for a vowel that lasts a tenth of a second.

## Looking at the result

Nobody has to listen to find the clips that are wrong. `review.py` draws every master as a spectrogram.

```
/tmp/ua-voice/.venv/bin/python tools/voice/review.py 'letter.*'
```

The sheets go in `assets/source/_scratch/voice-review`, which is not kept. A voiced sound is rows of bright bands that stay level or glide. Hiss is a smear high up, and a click is a thin vertical line. A clip that starts or ends with a bar, or has a smear through the middle of a vowel, is worth hearing. The clips marked `review` in `assets/source/voice/report.json` are the ones the generator itself was not sure of. After `pnpm assets:build`, `verify.py` has the listeners hear the files the game plays, and exits with 1 when one is not understood.

Every measure here is a stand-in for an ear. A recogniser, a formant, a pitch track and a spectrogram can all be fooled, and a voice can pass all of them and still sound odd. Nobody has yet heard the whole voice on a phone, so listen to it before anyone else does, and write down any clip that sounds wrong.

## From masters to the game

`tools/assets/speech.ts` turns each master into the file the game plays. It slows the master first, without changing its pitch, by the number in `PACE` (`src/shared/audio.ts`): 1.5 times as long for a sentence or an instruction (about 2.7 words a second where the masters run at 4), 1.25 times for a word or a shout, and not at all for the sound of a letter, which is a tenth of a second and would change what it is. Then it sets the same loudness for every clip (-12 LUFS by the loudest 400 ms, where it was -17 before, so the voice stands above the world's own sounds and not under them), holds the peak under -1 dBTP, encodes it as MP3 at 24 kHz and names it by the hash of what is in it. Some clips have one syllable much louder than the rest (`shout.apple` peaks 22 dB above its own average level), and a peak that tall would keep the whole clip quiet. A look-ahead limiter (`tools/audio/limiter.ts`) turns such a peak down by at most 6 dB, and the clip is turned up by the same, so 239 of the 2,556 clips are limited, 23 of them by the whole 6 dB and 69 by 3 dB or more. 120 clips end more than 0.3 LU under the target (the quietest is the piece `last.63` at -17.9 LUFS, which is about where every clip was before), and no whole clip is more than 0.3 LU above it; a piece of a sentence may be up to 1 LU over, as its check allows, and the packer turns down the few that slowing would push past half of that (`PIECE_MAX_OVER` in `tools/assets/speech.ts`). Three clips are held less, because the limiter spoils them: `word.bug` is not held at all, `shout.pumpkin` by 1.5 dB at most and `sound.ng` not at all (`HOLD_AT_MOST` in `tools/assets/speech.ts`). It writes the manifest's `speech` section, and `tools/assets/check/speech.ts` decodes every file and checks the size, the length (0.15 to 10 seconds, and the longest sentence is 8.12), the loudness, the peak, and that no clip begins or ends with a click. It decodes one file at a time with ffmpeg, which is about two minutes for the 2,556 clips on a quiet machine and four or five on a busy one, and `pnpm build` runs it before it bundles anything. The times of the words of a sentence (`src/content/reading/line-times.json`) are the masters' times multiplied by the same pace, and `pnpm voice:times` writes them.

The slowing is ffmpeg's `atempo`, which lays short pieces of the voice down further apart where they match. It does not change a master's words, but it can add a sound where a piece lands on the burst of a `g` or a `t`, and then the word listener writes `buggy` for `bug`. The limiter and the encoder can spoil a short word as well, so `verify.py` has the listeners hear the files the game plays, and it is what to run after any change to `PACE`, to the level or to the encoder. The first run heard the 1,201 words, shouts and letter sounds of the pack and did not understand 34. Seven of the 34 were not understood as masters either (`word.ant`, `word.are`, `word.chew`, `word.help`, `word.mice`, `word.sack` and `word.shack`) and they are as they were. The other 27 were understood as masters and spoiled by the pack, and the pack now treats each as it needs. 26 are said at the pace of their master (`KEPT_AT_MASTER_PACE` in `src/shared/audio.ts`, which holds two sentences more, below). Seven of those are also encoded at quality 2 and not 5 (`FINER` in `tools/assets/speech.ts`, about a kilobyte each), because a word a third of a second long has little to spare and the encoder at quality 5 turned `hole` into `hold` and `rib` into `ribby`. `word.bug` and `shout.pumpkin` are held less by the limiter, because a hold of 1.5 dB turns `bug` into `buggy` and a hold of 6 dB puts a thump under `pumpkin` that the check for a rattle hears (the share of its voice under 130 Hz goes from 0.18 with no hold to 0.51 with 6 dB). The 27th, `word.wall`, was a take on the edge, heard as `wow`, `whoa` or `well` from its own master, so it was made again with a long vowel (see `overrides.json`) and is slowed like the rest. The last run heard all 27 and understood them. The `num`, `term`, `maths` and `play` clips of the learning games were heard too, and all were understood but `num.6`, which the word listener wrote as `S-X` from its master and as `in 6` from the file that ships, with a puff of air before it. It is one of the five clips the generator marks for a person to look at, with `num.2`, `word.ant` and two sentences. The two clips added for the sums of Level 1, `term.times` and `term.divided-by`, were heard on the files that ship and understood (`times.` and `divided by...`). A sample of 150 sentences, instructions and cheers, chosen at random, was heard at the pace of 1.5 and all 150 were understood. A last run heard the other 868 clips, which are every sentence and instruction, the cheers and stars, the numbers and the sounds of letters and groups, and did not understand 6. The slowing had spoiled two sentences: `A hen in a pen.` was written `A hen in a pan.` and `I can dip a rib in it.` was written `I can dip a ribbon it.`, so both are said at the pace of their master now. The encoder and the limiter had spoiled four sounds, which the sound listener wrote as `ts.h` for `sound.ch`, `dʒ` for `sound.d`, nothing for `sound.ng` and `th` for `sound.t`. Those four are encoded at quality 2 now, and `sound.ng` is not held by the limiter, which leaves it 1.2 LU under the target. The same run found a sound in front of the `h` of `cheer.hooray` that the encoder had made, so it is encoded at quality 2 as well, which makes 12 clips in `FINER`. `verify.py` heard the seven again with the other 37 sounds, `cheer.wonderful` and `read.told`, and understood all 46. The last two have a sound before the word that the listener wrote for their masters too, and `verify.py` still lists them for a person to look at. The game fetches a clip when it is first wanted, decodes it, and holds a few seconds of the ones used most (`src/audio/voice-bank.ts`).

With the Qwen3 voice the pack is the same path, and the two listeners heard the shipped files again: every letter and sound, every clip marked `review`, and a fixed sample of 141 in all. All were understood but the three `review` words the recognisers fail as masters too (`word.an`, `word.shore`, `word.wore`), and one the pack had spoiled: the file of `word.make` was written "May" at three levels while its master is "Make". Slowing it cost the `k`, and the finer encoding alone did not save it; at its master's pace and the finer quality it is heard as "Make", so `word.make` is in both `KEPT_AT_MASTER_PACE` and `FINER`. The encoder also left the files of `word.chest`, `word.sheep` and `sound.sh` 0.4 LU louder than the packer set them, so those three are in `FINER` for their level. The first pack of the new voice failed the shipped-file check 21 times over: three sound masters under the 0.15 s floor, fourteen maths pieces over the piece ceiling, those three levels, and the pack 0.6 MiB over its budget. Each is fixed where it is made, and the check passes.
